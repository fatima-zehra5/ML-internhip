Capstone Research Paper

Can search-performance signals help identify content likely to experience an impressions decline?

A directional machine-learning experiment for prioritizing content review and monitoring.

Abstract

This study asks whether search-performance signals can help identify content items that may experience an impressions decline. The analysis uses aggregated and pseudonymized search-performance data, including daily content performance and query-level visibility signals. A Random Forest classifier is compared with a majority-class baseline using client-grouped validation so that testing includes previously unseen clients. The measured evaluation achieved 0.786 test accuracy and 0.754 macro F1 on 31,686 test rows from 12 held-out clients. The resulting output is intended for decision support, helping prioritize content for human review and monitoring rather than making causal claims or automatic search-engine decisions.

1. Introduction / Problem Statement

The decision supported by this analysis is which content items should receive review or monitoring when impressions may be declining. The unit of analysis is an aggregated content item. The output is a model-based risk signal that can be used to prioritize items for human review.

Data and machine learning are useful here because multiple search-performance signals can be combined into one repeatable prioritization workflow. A wrong call can either spend editorial effort on a low-priority item or cause a potentially important item to be overlooked. For that reason, the model is treated as decision support rather than an automatic action engine.

2. Data Safety & Data

The study uses the pseudonymized internship warehouse release. The analysis uses aggregated content-level and query-level information rather than exposing raw search records.

Tables used

  • fact_content_daily_performance — daily search-performance signals.
  • fact_content_query_90d — query-level visibility and concentration signals.
  • dim_clients — available client history used for grouping and understanding the data.

Analysis window

A 90-day analysis window was used with a minimum previous-period impression threshold of 200. Content items without sufficient history were excluded.

Safety and exclusions

Client names, domains, private queries, credentials and raw exports are excluded from the public analysis. The pseudonymous client identifier is used for grouping during validation and is not used as a predictive feature.

3. Methodology

Features

  • Previous-period impressions
  • Query visibility
  • Rare and anonymized impression shares
  • Top-query share
  • Ranking-position volatility

Target definition

A content item is classified as declining when its latest-period impressions are below 80% of its previous-period impressions.

Model

A Random Forest classifier is used as the predictive model. A majority-class rule is used as the transparent baseline.

Validation

Instead of a random row-level split, GroupShuffleSplit is used with client_hash_id as the grouping variable. This keeps clients separated between training and testing and provides a more realistic test of generalization to unseen clients.

Leakage checks

Features are calculated from information available before the outcome being evaluated. Label-derived fields are not used as predictive features. The pseudonymous client identifier is used only for grouping. Fixed random seeds support reproducibility.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The dataset is pseudonymized, so individual clients and domains cannot be identified.
  • The results describe associations in the available search-performance data and do not establish causation.
  • Performance may vary for clients or content outside the tested sample.
  • The evaluation covers 12 unseen clients, so the result should not be treated as a universal performance estimate.
  • The model identifies content that may warrant review; it does not guarantee that a recommended action will improve search performance.
  • Aggregated and anonymized query-level data limits visibility into detailed search behavior.

6. Ranked Recommendations

  1. Review high-risk declining content first. Prioritize content items that the model identifies as likely to decline.
  2. Investigate ranking-position volatility. Content with unstable position signals should receive additional monitoring.
  3. Review query concentration. Highly concentrated query visibility may indicate dependence on a small number of search queries.
  4. Prioritize sufficient historical evidence. Stronger historical evidence should be preferred over very low-volume content.
  5. Keep a human in the loop. Use the model as a review-prioritization tool rather than automatically triggering content changes.
